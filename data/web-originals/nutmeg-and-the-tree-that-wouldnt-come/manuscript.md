# Nutmeg and the Tree That Wouldn’t Come

SnackPack Christmas picture book · Ages 4–8 · 24 story pages plus cover

**Jacket copy**  
Nutmeg has picked the perfect little fir for the Christmas party. She has a cart, a wooden star and Corgi to help her. Then the fir holds on tight. With everyone expecting a tree in the square, Nutmeg has to look a little closer at the place where it grows.

### Cover art

Nutmeg crouches in the snowy library garden, a small wooden star dangling from her paw. The little fir in its old half-barrel leans over her. Amber light spills from a library window. Keep the fir plain and the outcome a surprise. Render the title separately in the web reader.

### Visual continuity

Nutmeg wears her green cable-knit jumper and cranberry scarf throughout. Corgi-on-the-Cob has orange-and-white fur and a teal scarf. Professor Hooten wears his established librarian outfit. The star is palm-sized, unpainted wood, with five rounded points and one red string loop. The little fir is about one and a half times Nutmeg’s height. Its half-barrel has one split slat. The split and root sit on the cart-facing side. A wide, level lane goes round the library to the garden gate. Its stone step is on the separate front path. Begin with frost-blue morning and end in early amber dusk. The fir stays rooted in the same place throughout the book.

## Page 01

The week before Christmas, Nutmeg shook the wooden star out of her mitten drawer. It had a dent in one point where she had dropped it when she was small. She put the red loop round her wrist.

**Illustration** — Close interior of the mitten drawer at squirrel height. Scarlet mittens and folded wool frame Nutmeg’s paw and the small wooden star with its dent. Crisp light through a frosted window.

## Page 02

“I’ve found our tree!” she called to Corgi. “It’s just the right size. This year even I can reach the top.” Out they went with the library’s little red cart. The wheels made two neat lines in the snow.

**Illustration** — Long shot from behind as Nutmeg pulls the empty red handcart and Corgi trots beside her. Two wheel lines draw the eye toward the snowy woodland library.

## Page 03

The fir stood in a wooden barrel behind the library. It smelled of cold rain and pine. One branch stuck straight out, like a paw waiting to shake. “Ready, fir?” said Nutmeg.

**Illustration** — Child-height first view of the tree. Nutmeg considers its one distinctive horizontal branch. Old slatted half-barrel on the earth near the library wall, light snow all around.

## Page 04

Corgi held the cart steady. Nutmeg took hold of the barrel. They counted to three. The cart bumped. The snow slipped. The barrel stayed exactly where it was.

**Illustration** — Low, playful action shot showing the empty cart bumping slightly as Nutmeg braces against the barrel and Corgi steadies it. Fir remains upright. Never show the tree on the cart.

## Page 05

“Perhaps it’s frozen to the ground,” said Corgi. He swept away the loose snow with one paw. Nutmeg brushed a little farther. Under the rim of the barrel, something brown curved into the earth.

**Illustration** — Over-shoulder discovery close-up, eyes and low paws at ground level. Reveal only the curved brown shape below the split slat and a small brushed patch in the snow.

## Page 06

It was a root. It had slipped out through a split in the wood. In the time it took Nutmeg to grow out of two pairs of mittens, the little fir had gone and planted itself.

**Illustration** — Clear root-level view of one visible root passing from the split slat into exposed soil. Nutmeg kneels beside it with wide eyes. The root is firm and undamaged.

## Page 07

Nutmeg let go of the barrel. “The whole square is waiting for this tree,” she said. She looked up at the green tips. She looked down at the little root. She put the star back in her pocket.

**Illustration** — Medium profile of Nutmeg quietly pocketing the star. Corgi watches her. Keep both fir tips and the half-barrel in the composition so her choice reads visually.

## Page 08

They walked round to the square. Professor Hooten was putting a low bench by the noticeboard. Panda-Cake had brought a tin of star biscuits. In the middle of the square was a big, swept circle. It looked terribly empty.

**Illustration** — Wide square with a conspicuous clean circle amid snow. Hooten beside a low bench near the noticeboard, Panda-Cake holding a closed biscuit tin, the friends arriving with an empty cart.

## Page 09

“The fir has roots,” Nutmeg told them. “I told everybody there would be a tree.” She rubbed the dent in her star through her pocket. Hooten set the bench down and listened.

**Illustration** — Eye-level grouping beside the bench. Nutmeg’s mittened or bare paw presses her jumper pocket, her ears tilted in worry. Hooten leans down. Use no dialogue lettering.

## Page 10

“We could make one out of broom handles,” said Corgi. He crossed two brooms and put his tail through the middle. Nutmeg laughed in spite of herself. Then both brooms slid over with a clack.

**Illustration** — Corgi sits between two fallen crossed brooms, looking surprised. Nutmeg laughs, the square still empty behind them. Make the physical gag readable.

## Page 11

Nutmeg looked from the empty square to the empty cart. “We were going to bring the tree to the party,” she said. She touched the cart’s cold handle. “What if the party could come to the tree?”

**Illustration** — Three-quarter shot with Nutmeg turning the cart toward the library. Let her change of posture carry the idea. Corgi’s ears rise. No imaginary thought bubbles.

## Page 12

They brought Hooten to see the garden. He liked the fir’s crooked branch. He liked the long wall that kept off the wind. Then he pointed to the stone step at the front. “How will we get my bench down that?”

**Illustration** — View from the front path showing the single stone step into the garden, with Hooten and his bench above the drop and the fir farther back. The tree stays in its barrel.

## Page 13

Corgi disappeared round the corner. A moment later his ears popped up on the other side of the garden gate. “There’s a lane back here,” he called. Nutmeg could just see his tail between two tall snowbanks.

**Illustration** — View from inside the garden toward the wooden gate. Corgi peeks between gate rails while a snowy level lane winds behind him. Long shadows, a hint of afternoon light.

## Page 14

The lane was wide enough for the cart, if they cleared the snow. Scritch went Hooten’s shovel. Shush went Nutmeg’s broom. Corgi tried to help with his tail. “You’re sweeping it straight at me,” said Nutmeg.

**Illustration** — Side view of teamwork on level ground. Hooten shovels, Nutmeg sweeps, and Corgi’s tail brushes snow in the wrong direction.

## Page 15

Hooten walked the whole lane with the loaded cart. It rolled through the gate without a bump. Nutmeg put fallen pinecones along the edge. Corgi carried one at a time, very carefully, as if each one were an egg.

**Illustration** — Long perspective of packed, cleared lane and cones along its edge. Corgi carries one cone. Show the small bench resting securely inside the red cart, Hooten guiding it through the open gate.

## Page 16

Back in the square, somebody had put up a long paper chain. Nutmeg and Corgi each took an end. They had nearly reached the garden when the wind caught the middle.

**Illustration** — Sustained tension in a long diagonal composition. The friends carry the paper chain between them along the cleared lane. A gust pulls its sagging middle sideways toward the gate.

## Page 17

Rip! The chain came apart. A red loop landed over Corgi’s nose. Nutmeg picked up three muddy loops and sat very still. For a moment she wished she had stayed home with her mittens.

**Illustration** — Low shot of the snapped chain and snowy muddy loops in Nutmeg’s lap. Corgi has a single intact red loop over his nose. He quietly sits beside her. Foreground detail rather than broad scenery.

## Page 18

Then Panda-Cake came round the corner with his biscuit tin. He sat down right there in the snow. “Did you know a broken star biscuit still tastes like cinnamon?” he said, and gave Nutmeg the bigger piece.

**Illustration** — Intimate seated scene of Panda-Cake sharing two pieces of one star-shaped biscuit. The torn paper lies to one side. Show his established apron, a winter wrap if supported by the reference plan, and a small round tin.

## Page 19

Nutmeg took a bite. “A little piece of paper chain will still look nice, too.” They rescued the dry parts. The longest went on the library door, the shortest on the back of Hooten’s bench.

**Illustration** — Nutmeg and Panda-Cake hang shorter salvaged strands under shelter by the library door and the bench. No new full-length chain appears. Corgi holds the end of a short strip on safe ground.

## Page 20

Then Nutmeg walked back to the square. By now there were neighbours in hats and boots, standing round the empty circle. She climbed onto the low step. “Our Christmas tree has settled in,” she said. “Follow my pinecones.”

**Illustration** — Eye-level gathering at the swept circle as Nutmeg speaks from a low step. Corgi waits at the entrance to the lane. Two or three simple woodland neighbours, each with a distinct silhouette.

## Page 21

The smallest neighbour spotted the next pinecone and hurried ahead. The slowest took Hooten’s arm. One by one they came through the garden gate. No one had to climb the stone step.

**Illustration** — Wide view inside the garden with guests arriving along the broad cleared lane. One small neighbour points to a pinecone and an older neighbour walks arm-in-arm with Hooten. Keep the gate and lane legible.

## Page 22

The fir was quite small with everyone around it. Nutmeg found she could reach the crooked branch without standing on tiptoe. She pulled the star out of her pocket and slipped its red loop over the green needles.

**Illustration** — Climax close-up of Nutmeg hanging the same wooden star at eye height on the distinctive crooked branch. Her feet are flat on the ground. Neighbours are softly suggested, not crowded around her paws.

## Page 23

At first the wooden star looked very plain. Then Hooten switched on the garden lantern. A little light caught in the dent. Panda-Cake opened his tin. Somebody began a song, and Nutmeg knew the second line.

**Illustration** — Warm dusk group in the sheltered garden. One electric lantern sends amber light across the tiny dented star and the biscuit tin is open. Snowy blue edge and warmly lit fir. Same fir and barrel placement.

## Page 24

Later, when only the little footprints were left, Nutmeg came back for her cart. A dusting of snow lay on the branch above the star. “See you tomorrow, fir,” she said. And she left the star just where it was.

**Illustration** — Quiet final wide composition echoing page three. Nutmeg leads the empty cart away and looks back. Fir and single wooden star glow near the library. A dusting of fresh snow and many footprints remain.
