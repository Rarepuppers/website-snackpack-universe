# Vincent Van Mouse and the Christmas Shadow Show

SnackPack Christmas picture book · Ages 4–8 · 24 story pages plus cover

**Jacket copy**  
Vincent has painted the whole village for the library’s Christmas window. Then a gust tears a hole right through his favourite picture. With the audience already on its way, a lamp and a scrap of card give him an idea for a show nobody has seen before.

### Cover art

From the audience side, a huge star shadow hangs above a row of roof shapes on a glowing cream cloth. Vincent peeks around the curtain with a proud smile. The library hall is honey-gold against the winter blue outside. The reader sets the title separately.

### Visual continuity

Vincent is the sandy mouse with round glasses, green eyes, cream shirt and paint-speckled blue work apron in his reference. His red paint spot appears on his nose from page 3 through the end. Panda-Cake wears his familiar pancake wrap and apron. Professor Hooten wears his established librarian outfit. The original artwork is a long, wide sheet showing snowy roofs and a crooked chimney in watercolour. It has a single central tear from page 7 onward, stopping above the bottom edge; it never separates into two sheets. The picture’s lower strip can still be cut into a rooftop silhouette. For the show, use one cream sheet, one warm electric desk lamp and small cardboard puppets behind the sheet. Children sit on the other side. The shadow side and audience side are opposite. Never show two lights or a shadow that contradicts the positions of the light and puppet.

## Page 01

Vincent Van Mouse had painted six chimneys, four puddles and the little bakery door. He paused over a roof that he knew by heart. His own house had just enough room for a crooked chimney and a mouse.

## Page 02

The picture was for the library’s Christmas window. Tonight, when the snow made the streets go blue, everyone would come to see it. Vincent imagined them finding their houses. He wondered who would spot his first.

**Illustration** — Side view of Vincent and the intact panoramic painting, looking toward the empty deep-silled library window from inside. A couple of small evergreen decorations, understated. Late-afternoon blue outdoors.

## Page 03

Panda-Cake brought a tin of biscuits for the audience. “There’s a bit of red on your nose,” he told him. Vincent rubbed his cheek. “Other side.” He rubbed his chin. Panda-Cake handed him a clean rag, which he forgot to use.

**Illustration** — Character close-up, red paint dot clearly on Vincent’s nose. Panda-Cake offers a cloth, a closed round biscuit tin behind him on the table. Gentle visual humour about where Vincent is wiping.

## Page 04

Together they lifted the long painting. “I’ll hold this end,” said Panda-Cake. “You hold yours.” Vincent trotted backwards towards the window. The picture sagged in the middle, like a washing line.

**Illustration** — Wide interior shot of the two carrying opposite edges of the same painting between the worktable and the window. The middle sags visibly. Keep the route and furniture clear for the page turn.

## Page 05

Professor Hooten opened the hall door to bring in the chairs. Cold air rushed past him. The small paper stars on the shelf trembled. Then the great sheet of painted paper gave a shiver of its own.

**Illustration** — From beside the hall door, Hooten holds it open beside a chair. A cold gust lifts small cut paper stars and makes the long intact painting bow. No visible wind markings or motion comics.

## Page 06

“Hold it there!” cried Vincent. He gripped his end with both paws. Panda-Cake held his. The gust pushed the middle up and over the corner of the window latch.

**Illustration** — Close view of the sharp silhouette of the latch catching the bowed wet paper while Vincent and Panda-Cake hold both ends. No tear visible yet.

## Page 07

Rrrrip. A rip ran down the Christmas street, stopping just above the bottom edge. Half of Vincent’s chimney folded back. He could see Panda-Cake through the hole where his house ought to have been.

**Illustration** — View through the torn central opening to Panda-Cake’s concerned face. One floppy flap shows part of a crooked chimney. Make the tear dramatic with paper fibres but keep the expressions gentle.

## Page 08

Hooten shut the door. “That was a big gust,” he said softly. Vincent put the painting on the table. He tried to press the chimney flat. His paw was shaking, so Panda-Cake set the rag beside him and waited.

**Illustration** — Quiet close scene at the table, torn painting flat except for its flap. Vincent tries to smooth it, Hooten by the shut door, Panda-Cake’s paw setting down the white rag. No joke on this page.

## Page 09

They tried to mend the rip from behind. The two sides almost met. But the paint had stuck to the latch and come away, leaving a white stripe down the wall of Vincent’s house. He tucked his brush inside his apron.

**Illustration** — Close overhead of the mended but scarred watercolour. Only a single irregular white stripe where pigment lifted. Vincent’s brush going into the blue apron pocket at the bottom of frame.

## Page 10

Outside, one child stood on tiptoe to peer through the glass. Another was brushing snow off the window ledge with a mitten. Vincent could see them. He slid down in his chair so they couldn’t see him.

**Illustration** — From inside the blue twilight window, two bundled animal children outside, one peering in and one brushing the ledge. Vincent crouches behind the window-height chair. Keep the contrast cosy.

## Page 11

Panda-Cake took a cardboard star from the shelf. “We could put a star over the white bit.” The desk lamp was already on. When he picked up the star, its shadow swept over the wall.

**Illustration** — View across the craft table, showing the lit electric lamp, Panda-Cake lifting one small cardboard star between the light and wall, and its single large dark shape crossing the wall. Torn painting remains below.

## Page 12

“Hold it there,” said Vincent. This time he said it very quietly. He watched the star grow when Panda-Cake moved it towards the lamp. When he moved back, it shrank. He climbed out of his chair.

**Illustration** — Medium shot of Vincent climbing down, face bright with attention, watching one enlarged shadow. Shadow corresponds to the star nearer the lamp. Do not depict two time states in one picture.

## Page 13

Vincent snipped round the torn flap and lifted it. On the wall, the scrap became a steep little roof. He tipped it sideways, and there was his crooked chimney. “There you are,” he said.

**Illustration** — Clear optical silhouette of the crooked chimney on the wall. Vincent holds up the corresponding cut or torn-off painted scrap between the desk lamp and wall. Let his recognition tell the story.

## Page 14

“Hooten, may we borrow a sheet?” asked Vincent. “And could the chairs face this way?” He pointed away from the window, towards the warm end of the hall, where two low bookcases stood with a gap between them.

**Illustration** — Wider hall geography, Vincent points to a gap between two sturdy waist-high bookcases at one end of the hall. Hooten holds one chair in the foreground. Keep the window off to the side.

## Page 15

Hooten clipped a plain sheet to two tall poles behind the bookcases and set the lamp on a table behind it. Panda-Cake walked round to the front. Vincent lifted his roof. A house popped up on the sheet. “I can see it from here!” he called.

**Illustration** — Audience-side three-quarter angle of taut cream fabric and a projected crooked-roof silhouette. Show Panda-Cake on the viewing side and only a hint of the lamp table round the fabric edge. Lamp and cutout stay hidden to the audience.

## Page 16

Vincent took his little scissors. From the snowy strip below the rip, he snipped a new row of roofs. Panda-Cake cut a cardboard squirrel with a parcel under its arm.

**Illustration** — Overhead craft-table composition. Vincent cuts a new rooftop outline from the snowy-blue strip below the rip, using blunt scissors. The remaining panorama retains the cut-out roof opening and white scar. Panda-Cake’s simple squirrel silhouette has one parcel shape. Lamp and set are in the background.

## Page 17

The squirrel looked very fine from the front, until Panda-Cake tried to make it walk. Its parcel bumped a roof. Its tail went up a chimney. “That’s the wrong way home,” said Vincent, laughing for the first time since the rip.

**Illustration** — One comic action from the audience side. The squirrel shadow’s tail comically overlaps the crooked chimney shape. In a sliver offstage, show Vincent laughing, or let his rounded ears peek around the curtain edge.

## Page 18

They tried again. This time the squirrel stood farther from the lamp, beside the sheet. Small enough! It trotted down the street between the roofs. Vincent practised making the star rise. Panda-Cake practised three little taps.

**Illustration** — Backstage rehearsal showing the moving squirrel cutout closer to the sheet and the lamp farther behind. A spoon and tin wait near Panda-Cake. Balance foreground real props with the glowing cloth.

## Page 19

Hooten went out to the waiting children. “The Christmas picture is ready,” he said. “Come inside and find a chair.” Behind the sheet, Vincent heard boots tapping and two last chairs scraping along the floor.

**Illustration** — From the warm hall doorway, Hooten welcomes the two bundled children from page ten and a few neighbours. Rows of low chairs look toward the bright sheet. Nobody crosses the backstage lighting area.

## Page 20

Panda-Cake tapped his spoon on the biscuit tin. Ting. Ting. Ting. The hall went quiet. Vincent raised the little street. On the sheet, rooftops grew out of a pool of gold.

**Illustration** — Audience perspective. Children in the lower foreground, one illuminated screen reflecting on their faces. Simple, clearly legible silhouette of a street including Vincent’s crooked chimney. No physical puppets visible on this side.

## Page 21

A squirrel travelled from house to house. At the last door, she set down her parcel and knocked. Then up came the star. A small voice in the front row whispered, “That’s Vincent’s chimney.”

**Illustration** — Main performance hero scene, audience side. Full simple tableau of dark roofs, small squirrel at last door, a round-pointed star above the crooked chimney. One young viewer leans towards a friend.

## Page 22

Vincent almost forgot to move the star. He had thought his chimney was ruined. Here it was, as crooked as ever, big enough for the whole room to see. He held the star steady while the audience clapped.

**Illustration** — Backstage close-up of Vincent smiling, red nose dot still there. He stands beside the warm desk lamp, holding a cutout on a thin stick, with the roof shape mounted in place. Let the colour and pose convey relief.

## Page 23

Afterwards the children took turns with the puppets. One made the squirrel as big as a house. One put the star in the squirrel’s paws. Panda-Cake opened the biscuit tin. Vincent looked down and found another red spot on his apron.

**Illustration** — After-show play, supervised with Hooten present. A child controls the squirrel puppet safely away from the electric lamp. Another tries a star near the sheet. Panda-Cake opens the tin and Vincent examines his new apron spot.

## Page 24

He turned off the lamp and carried the crooked little house to the window. Outside, the snow had stopped. The silhouette sat on the sill beside the last biscuit. Vincent licked a crumb off his paw. “Good night, little house.”

**Illustration** — Quiet close on the library window and sill. Crooked-chimney paper cutout beside one remaining biscuit. Vincent licks a crumb from his paw. Snow outside has stopped, sky is deep indigo, and the desk lamp is off.
