Animal field notes

Meet the animals

Explore the real animals that inspire SnackPack characters.

Blue jaysBlue jays eat acorns and may store some to eat later.Bottlenose dolphinsA bottlenose dolphin breathes through a blowhole on top of its head.Brown bearsBrown bears use strong claws to dig up food such as roots.CentipedesA centipede has one pair of legs on each segment that carries legs.ChickensChickens take dust baths to help keep clean.DogsDogs have a well-developed sense of smell. Some working dogs learn to pick out a particular scent.ElephantsAn elephant’s trunk joins its nose and upper lip. The trunk has no bones.Giant pandaA special wrist bone helps a giant panda grip bamboo as it eats.GiraffesGiraffes are the tallest animals that live on land.MiceHouse mice use their whiskers to feel the surfaces they explore.OctopusesAn octopus has eight arms. The arms carry suckers that help it grip.OwlsSpecial feather edges help many owls fly with very little sound.PenguinsA penguin pushes through the water with its flippers and steers with its feet and tail.Rabbits and haresRabbits and hares can turn their long ears in different directions to listen.SharksA shark’s skeleton is made of cartilage, the same kind of flexible material found in the tip of your nose.TigersEach tiger has its own stripe pattern.Tree squirrelsEastern gray squirrels bury food in little stores to eat through winter.TurtlesA turtle’s shell is part of its body. It is joined to its spine and ribs.PigsPigs use their strong snouts to dig for food such as roots and insects.Red foxesRed foxes can live in towns and cities. They use sheltered places for dens.HedgehogsA hedgehog can curl into a ball so its spines face outwards.ButterfliesA butterfly starts as an egg. It hatches into a caterpillar and changes into an adult inside a pupa.Bald eaglesAdult bald eagles have white feathers on their heads and tails. Young bald eagles start out mostly brown.RaccoonsA raccoon can sleep in a hollow tree, under a log or in an old burrow.Horses and poniesA horse’s hooves keep growing, so a horse in human care needs regular hoof checks and trimming.EarthwormsMany earthworms live in soil and shelter in burrows. Some come up to the surface to move around.LeopardsA leopard’s long tail helps it balance on narrow branches.LadybirdsLadybirds are beetles. Many eat tiny plant-feeding insects called aphids.LionsLions can live in groups called prides. Lionesses in a pride often raise their cubs together.DeerDeer that grow antlers shed them and grow a new set.SpidersA spider’s eight legs attach to the front part of its body.GoatsGoats chew their food again after first swallowing it. This helps them break down tough plants.SnakesA snake can flick its tongue to pick up tiny scent particles from around it.Ball pythonsA ball python may curl into a tight ball and hide its head when it feels threatened.HamstersA golden hamster can carry food in pouches inside its cheeks.ParrotsParrots have two toes pointing forwards and two pointing backwards. These feet help them grip branches.Fennec foxesA fennec fox’s large ears help it hear and release heat in the desert.Long-necked dinosaursSauropods were plant-eating dinosaurs with long necks. Dough Mino is an imaginary dinosaur inspired by them.Domestic catsA cat’s whiskers sense nearby objects and movement in the air.
