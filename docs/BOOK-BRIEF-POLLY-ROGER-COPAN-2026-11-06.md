# Web-exclusive book brief: Polly Roger and the Red Wings of Copán

Status: published 9 November 2026 at /read/polly-roger-and-the-red-wings-of-copan/, on Mark's
instruction and before the Honduran read-through, which is still wanted (see "Before
publishing"). Page text now lives in `data/web-books.json`; change it there. The Spanish
version below is NOT published.

## Why this book

- A class in Honduras began using `/read/` on 2 November 2026. That it is one class is an
  inference from device, time and search patterns, and the class has not been in touch.
- Phonics-led books lead the shelf: Pip Sits, Quill-Berry, Ten Red Hens, Meow-inara and
  Nan and Pip are all in the 28-day top ten. No four-page short read reached the top 25.
- So this book is written at Reading Trail level, using -ing, -ed and -er endings and
  vowel teams, rather than as another general short read.

## Book details

- **Character:** Polly Roger, already on the shelf. She is drawn as a red macaw in a purple
  and gold coat with a gold feathered hat, and she keeps that outfit here. It is how a child
  tells her from Rosa. She adds a small travel bag.
- **New character:** Rosa, a scarlet macaw and an exchange student from Honduras at Wise
  Owl's school. Readers in Honduras get a character from home, and her school clothes and
  backpack set her apart from Polly.
- **Older companion:** `docs/BOOK-BRIEF-ROSAS-COPAN-REPORT-2026-11-06.md`.
- **Length:** six pages, matching the Talk together and end-question format of the shelf.
- **Theme:** people and birds looking after a place together.
- **Phonics focus:** -ing, -ed and -er endings; vowel teams ee, ea, ai, oa.

**End questions**

1. What is a red macaw called in Honduras? *A guara roja.*
2. Why do people put nest boxes in the trees? *To help the macaws come back to the valley.*

**Word list for the book page:** macaw, valley, carved, vanished, nest box, baleada.

## Spanish version

A side-by-side Spanish text would make the book far more useful to a Honduran class.
This draft must be checked by a Honduran Spanish speaker before use.

1. Rosa es una estudiante de intercambio en la escuela de Wise Owl. Es de Honduras. Hoy lleva a su amiga Polly Roger a su tierra.
2. Vuelan sobre verdes colinas. —En Honduras, a un ave como nosotras la llamamos guara roja —dice Rosa—. ¡Yo soy una, y tú también!
3. Rosa lleva a Polly a Copán. Hace mucho tiempo, los mayas construyeron esta ciudad de piedra. Polly se detiene. —¡Mira! ¡Un pájaro de piedra me está mirando!
4. —Los mayas tallaron guaras en la piedra —dice Rosa—. Pero las guaras de verdad casi desaparecieron de este valle. Entonces la gente empezó a ayudar.
5. Rosa le muestra a Polly un nido de madera en lo alto de un árbol. Dentro pían dos polluelos. —Estamos volviendo —dice Rosa.
6. Al atardecer, comen juntas una baleada caliente. Unas alas rojas vuelan sobre las viejas piedras. Polly sonríe. —Este es el mejor tesoro.

## Facts behind the story

| Claim | Source | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Scarlet macaw is the national bird, declared 28 June 1993 | [10,000 Birds](https://10000birds.com/what-is-the-national-bird-of-honduras.htm), [A-Z Animals](https://a-z-animals.com/blog/scarlet-macaws-national-bird-of-honduras/) | High |
| Macaws nearly vanished from the Copán Valley and were reintroduced, with nests installed at the site | [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/scarlet-macaw-recovery-national-bird-honduras-180974740/), [La Prensa](https://www.laprensa.hn/honduras/guacamaya_roja-guacamaya-copan-ave_nacional-honduras-KCLP1074853) | High; dates vary, so the book gives none |
| Stone macaw heads at the Copán ball court | [FLAAR](https://flaar-mesoamerica.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Copan-maya-ballcourt-architecture-Scarlet-macaw-logo-goalstones-photos-forweb-2023-10-04-NH.pdf), [Maya Ruins](https://mayaruins.com/copan/a1_1079.html) | High |
| Baleada: folded flour tortilla with beans, cheese and cream | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baleada) | High; origin stories disputed, so the book avoids them |

Note that baleadas are most associated with the north coast, not Copán, though they are
eaten across the country. The reviewer should confirm that page 6 feels right.

## Art rules

- Use reference photos of the Copán ball court and scarlet macaws. No invented temples,
  no Aztec or generic jungle-temple designs.
- No human figures in traditional dress. The story has no people on the page, which avoids
  caricature.
- Polly must match `read/polly-rogers-pirate-cove/pages/` and the portrait
  `apps/snackpack-3-spelling-sentences/assets/character-portraits/polly-roger-parrot.webp`.
- Full art instructions are in `docs/ART-BRIEF-WEB-BOOKS-2026-11-06.md`.
