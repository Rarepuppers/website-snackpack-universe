How to learn to type

Touch typing means typing without looking at the keys. It feels slow at first, because your fingers have to learn where every key lives, but it pays back for life: children who touch type write longer stories, get less tired, and keep their eyes on the screen instead of hunting for letters. Snack Typer teaches it the way typing teachers do, one row of the keyboard at a time.

Every lesson starts from the home row. Rest your left fingers on A, S, D and F, and your right fingers on J, K, L and the semicolon. Feel for the small bumps on F and J: they tell your index fingers where home is without looking. Each finger reaches up or down for its own keys and then comes back home. On the keyboard under the text, every key is coloured by the finger that presses it, and the next key to press is outlined.

The lessons

  • Home row: words made only from A S D F G H J K L, such as ask, glad and salad.
  • Top row: adds Q W E R T Y U I O P, so words like kite, water and purple become possible.
  • Bottom row: adds Z X C V B N M for zebra, box and snack.
  • All letters: words that use the awkward letters on purpose: quiz, jelly, kayak.
  • Story sentences: real lines from our free decodable readers, like “The cat sat in a hat.” They bring in capital letters and full stops. Each one says which book it came from, so you can go and read the whole story.
  • Longer sentences: lines from the longer picture books, with names, apostrophes and commas.

Tips for parents and teachers

  • Accuracy first. The stars count accuracy, not speed. Three stars means 98% or better. Speed comes once the fingers know the way.
  • Short and often. One lesson takes about a minute. Five minutes a day beats an hour on Saturday.
  • Cover the hands. Once a child knows the home row, a tea towel over the hands stops peeking.
  • Use the daily. Today's daily is the same for everyone, so a class can compare scores on the same words. There is more for classrooms on our page for teachers.

Frequently asked questions

Is Snack Typer free?

Yes. It plays free in your browser with no ads, no sign-in and nothing to install.

What age is it for?

About six and up. The first lessons use short words from the home row, and the sentence lessons come from decodable readers for children who are learning to read.

Why do the stars count accuracy, not speed?

Children who look at the keys or guess learn habits that slow them down later. Speed follows once the right finger finds the right key. Your best speed is still kept for each lesson.

Can I use it in a classroom?

Yes. Pupils do not sign in and nothing personal is collected. Records stay in each browser. Share the daily link and the whole class types the same words that day.

Does it work on a phone or tablet?

Yes, with the on-screen keyboard, but it teaches touch typing best with a real keyboard. Tap the text box to start typing.
