How to learn math facts

Math facts are the small sums children use thousands of times: 7 + 8, 15 − 6, 6 × 7, 42 ÷ 6. When they are instant, a child's attention is free for the real problem — the word problem, the long multiplication, the fraction. When they are not, every step stalls while fingers count. Fact Sprint gives short, focused practice on exactly these facts, one family at a time.

There are two ways to play. Practice asks 20 questions at your own pace. If you miss a fact, the right answer appears, and the same fact comes back a few questions later so you can get it right while it is fresh. Stars are for accuracy: three stars means every answer right in the end, with no more than one slip. Sprint is a 60-second race: how many can you answer correctly? Your best score is kept for each lesson, so you can watch it grow day by day.

The lessons

  • Add to 10: the number bonds every later sum is built on. Dots under each question help children who still count.
  • Add to 20: sums like 8 + 7, where "make ten" is the trick: 8 + 2 is 10, and 5 more is 15.
  • Subtract to 20: the same facts in reverse. Thinking "what do I add?" makes them easier.
  • Times tables: pick one table from 2× to 12×, or Mixed once a few are secure.
  • Division: the times tables backwards: 42 ÷ 6 asks "six times what is 42?"
  • Mix: all four operations together, for children who know them all.

Tips for parents and teachers

  • One family at a time. Stay on a lesson until it is three stars before moving on.
  • Little and often. A practice round takes two or three minutes. A few rounds a day beat one long session.
  • Read a number story. Each lesson suggests one of our free illustrated number stories that uses the same idea, with a question on every page.
  • Use the daily in class. Today's daily is the same 20 questions for everyone, so pupils can compare. There is more on our page for teachers.

Frequently asked questions

Is Fact Sprint free?

Yes. It plays free in your browser with no ads, no sign-in and nothing to install.

Which facts does it cover?

Addition to 10 and to 20, subtraction to 20, the 2 to 12 times tables one at a time or mixed, and the matching division facts.

Why does a missed fact come back?

In Practice, a fact you miss returns a few questions later. Getting it right soon after a mistake is how facts stick.

Can a class use it?

Yes. Pupils do not sign in and nothing personal is collected. Records stay in each browser. The daily set is the same for everyone each day.

Does it work on a tablet?

Yes. The on-screen number pad has large buttons, and a keyboard works too.
