How to play Big Two

Big Two is a climbing card game for four players with a standard 52-card deck. Everyone gets 13 cards, and the first player to play them all wins. It is widely played across East and Southeast Asia, where you may know it as Dai Di, Pusoy Dos or Deuces. House rules vary from one table to the next, so this page sets out exactly the rules used here.

Cards rank from 3 (lowest) up to King, then Ace, then 2 (highest). When two cards share a rank, the suit decides: diamonds are lowest, then clubs, hearts and spades. That makes the 3 of diamonds the lowest card in the deck and the 2 of spades the highest. Whoever holds the 3 of diamonds leads the first round and must include it in their play.

What you can play

  • Single — one card.
  • Pair — two cards of the same rank. The higher card in the pair decides who wins.
  • Three of a kind — three cards of the same rank.
  • Five-card hand — from weakest to strongest: straight, flush, full house, four of a kind (with any fifth card), straight flush.

You must always match the number of cards on the table. A pair can only be beaten by a higher pair, and a five-card hand only by a stronger five-card hand. Any flush beats any straight, and any full house beats any flush. When two hands are the same type, straights and flushes are compared by their highest card, and full houses and four of a kind are compared by the rank of the group.

Passing takes you out of the current round. When three players have passed, whoever played last clears the table and leads anything they like.

How to win more often

  • Plan your five-card hands first. They get rid of the most cards at once. Build pairs from what is left.
  • Lead low when you win a round. A cheap five-card hand or a low pair clears your weak cards while the other players still hold their strong ones.
  • Keep a 2 for control. A 2 usually wins the round and lets you choose the next play. Spending one early can leave you with no way back.
  • Watch the card counts. When a player is down to one card, lead pairs or five-card hands so they cannot follow.
  • Use the Sort button. It switches between rank order and suit order, which makes flushes easier to spot.

Worked example: CPU 2 leads a straight, 4-5-6-7-8. You hold a flush in hearts, so you can beat it even though your top card is only a 10. A higher straight such as 10-J-Q-K-A would also beat it. When the other three players pass, you lead the next round.

Big Two and Thirteen

If you already play Thirteen (Tiến Lên), Big Two will feel familiar: the 2 is still the top card and you still race to empty your hand. The main difference is the shapes. Thirteen rewards long runs and bombs. Big Two is built around five-card poker hands, and you must always match the number of cards on the table.

Frequently asked questions

Is Big Two free here?

Yes. It plays free in your browser with no ads, no sign-in and nothing to install.

Is Big Two the same as Pusoy Dos?

They belong to the same family, and house rules differ. Some tables rank suits differently or allow other straights, so check the rules table above if you are used to another version.

Who do I play against?

Three computer players at the level you choose. Easy plays its lowest legal cards, Normal plans its leads, and Sharp also holds back its 2s until they matter. Each level keeps its own win–loss record on this device.

What's the Daily deal?

Turn on Daily and everyone gets the same cards that day, so you can compare results with friends. Starting a New game leaves Daily mode.

Why can't I play four cards?

Plays are always one, two, three or five cards. Four of a kind counts only as a five-card hand, with any fifth card added.
