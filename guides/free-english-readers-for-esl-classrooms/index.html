Free English readers for ESL classrooms

Short illustrated stories for pupils learning English, free to read online or print, with no sign-in and no ads.

Why decodable readers can help

Decodable books limit their words to letter-sound patterns a child has already met. That gives second-language learners a way into reading aloud. There is one thing to plan for: a pupil can sound out a word correctly without knowing what it means, so these books work best with some vocabulary teaching around them.

A simple lesson shape

  1. Before reading: look at the pictures and teach three or four key words with gestures or objects.
  2. First read: read the story aloud, or use Read to me on a device, while pupils follow.
  3. Second read: pupils read in pairs. Use “One page at a time” on shared screens.
  4. After reading: ask pupils to point to a word, retell a page, or answer the questions in the book.

Which books to choose

  • Beginners: Pip Sits and Nan and Pip use a small, repeated set of words.
  • Building sounds: Ten Red Hens and Bug in a Cup move on to more letters.
  • Practising spelling patterns: Meow-inara’s Vowel-Team Duet and Quill-Berry’s Ending Clues focus on vowel teams and word endings.

Sounds that may need extra practice

Which English sounds are hard depends on the home language. Listen for the sounds your pupils mix up, then use the sound cards for short practice before reading. The phonics order guide explains why the books introduce letters in this order.

Getting the books to your class

Our page for teachers has links and QR codes to share, and tips for classrooms with poor internet or no devices.

Frequently asked questions

Are these books free for classroom use?

Yes. Every book can be read online or downloaded as an A4 PDF at no cost, with no sign-in.

Do pupils need an account?

No. Pupils open the page in a browser and start reading.

Are the books available in other languages?

Not at the moment. The stories are in English.

What age are the books for?

They were written for young children learning to read. Older beginners can use them too, but check that the story feels right for your class.
