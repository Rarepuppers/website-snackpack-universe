How to play Last Bastion, a free browser roguelite

Most free browser games ask for ninety seconds. Last Bastion asks for twenty minutes and gives you a full roguelite in return: a route to choose, a build to assemble, five damage types that interact, and a boss at the end that will punish a build with no answer to armour. This guide covers what the game does not stop to explain.

The shape of a run

You hold a position against waves that arrive faster and tougher than you can clear them. That is deliberate: from the third wave onward you are not meant to kill everything. Waves end on a timer, so surviving is the win condition and killing is how you fund the next upgrade.

Every level-up offers a choice of cards. Every cleared node offers a reward. The run is the sum of those decisions, and a run that ends badly is usually a run where three separate choices each made sense alone.

Two ways to play

  • Quick Drop — one continuous fight against escalating waves. The fastest way to learn the controls and test a weapon you have not used.
  • Expedition — a twenty-node map across eight columns. You pick a route, carry your build from node to node, and finish at a regional boss. This is the real game.

Both are free, both are in the browser, and neither needs an account.

1. Damage types are the whole build

There are five: physical, fire, shock, cryo and toxic. The first four elements do something extra once you have applied enough of them to a target. Build up 40 points and the status triggers — fire leaves a burn, shock briefly stuns, cryo slows hard, and toxic both damages over time and strips armour.

The practical consequence is that spreading damage across every type is usually worse than committing to two. Buildup does not carry between types. A weapon dealing a little of everything triggers nothing; two weapons dealing the same element reach the threshold constantly. When a level-up offers you a card that deepens what you already have against one that adds a new element, the deepening is almost always correct.

One important exception: some elites adapt. Ironhide in particular counters repeated single-type damage, so a build with exactly one answer will eventually meet the enemy designed to ignore it. Two committed types, not one and not five.

2. Armour is why your damage stopped working

Enemy armour is not a flat subtraction. Percentage armour follows a diminishing curve, roughly armour / (armour + 15), so the first few points matter much more than the tenth. A separate flat reduction is then applied after the percentage, with a floor so that no hit is ever fully absorbed.

This is why a build that felt strong on wave four feels useless on wave nine. Armour rises with the wave number, and a weapon firing many small hits loses proportionally far more to flat reduction than one firing few large ones. If your damage has fallen off a cliff, you need either armour shred — toxic, most directly — or fewer, bigger hits.

3. Read the telegraph, not the enemy

Anything that will hurt you meaningfully announces itself first: a ground ring before a slam, reticles before a volley, a filling arc before a sweep. The tell is always longer than the reaction it needs. Learning four or five telegraphs matters more than any weapon you will draft, because the damage that ends runs is almost never the trickle from ordinary enemies.

Mini-bosses resist hard crowd control, so cryo will slow them but never simply stop them. Plan to move rather than to lock them down.

Choosing a route

The expedition map shows what each node holds before you commit. Hover or focus a node to preview it; the first press arms the route and a second press actually deploys, so you cannot lose a run to a misclick.

  • Supply depots and weapon caches are safe. A route with none of them is a route that arrives at the boss underfed.
  • Elites guarantee a cache. Worth the risk once your build is working, rarely worth it in the first two columns.
  • Mini-bosses pay in arsenal. They never appear before the third column, and a depot always comes first.
  • Objective nodes — escort, deny, collect — pay a relic from a pool you cannot get any other way.

Five mistakes that end early runs

  1. Drafting for variety. Five damage types means five thresholds you never reach.
  2. Standing still to kill. Waves end on a timer. You do not need the last enemy.
  3. Ignoring armour until it is too late. By the time damage feels bad, the cards that fix it are three levels back.
  4. Taking every elite. The cache is real, and so is arriving at the boss at a sliver of health.
  5. Treating the escort like a kill room. The objective moves whether you are ready or not.

Five characters, and what they actually change

Marine, Medic, Assault, Tactician and Scout are not reskins. Each carries a different number of upgrade slots by category — offensive, defensive, support, scavenger — and that slot profile, more than the starting weapon, decides what a run can become. A hero with two defensive slots simply cannot build the same way as one with four.

Start as the Marine. It has the most forgiving slot spread and the clearest starting weapon, and every lesson above transfers.

Difficulty, when you want it

Four threat tiers change what the map sends at you rather than just inflating numbers: Standard, Elite Patrols, Rapid Incursion and the tier beyond. Raising the tier is the intended way to keep a mastered build interesting, and it is a better answer to boredom than a new weapon.

Play it now

No download, no account, no adverts

Last Bastion runs in the browser on desktop and plays with either keyboard or gamepad — including the menus, the map and the level-up screens, so a controller never strands you on a screen it cannot operate.

Progress is saved in your own browser rather than on our server: unlocks, run history and an expedition in progress all survive closing the tab. There is no login because there is nothing to log in to. The settings screen can export a backup file if you want to keep a save, and it will tell you plainly if your browser is blocking storage rather than pretending the save worked.

Common questions

Is it really free?

Yes. No adverts, no account, no purchases, nothing gated behind a payment. It is a free browser game from a small studio.

Does it work on a phone?

It is built and tested for desktop with keyboard or gamepad. It will load on a phone, but the controls assume a keyboard or a controller, so the experience is much better on a computer.

Will I lose my save?

Only if you clear your browser's site data for this site, or play in a private window. The save lives in your own browser. Export a backup from the settings screen if that worries you.

How long is a run?

A Quick Drop is a few minutes. A full expedition across all eight columns to the boss is usually fifteen to twenty-five minutes, and you can close the tab mid-run and resume.
