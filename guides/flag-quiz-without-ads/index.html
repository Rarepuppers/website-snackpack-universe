A flag quiz without ads or lookalike traps

Flag quizzes are the easiest geography quiz to make and one of the easiest to make unfair. A handful of national flags are so close to one another that telling them apart at quiz size is a test of pixel-peering rather than knowledge — while others look confusable and are actually easy once you know the one detail that separates them. Knowing which is which is most of the skill.

The pairs that are genuinely hard, and the ones that only look it

Genuinely near-identical. Chad and Romania both fly blue-yellow-red vertical tricolours; the difference is a slightly darker blue on Chad's, and at quiz size it is close to invisible. Monaco and Indonesia are both a red bar over a white bar, differing in proportion rather than design. These are the pairs where a fair quiz either shows both together and asks you to distinguish them deliberately, or accepts that it is testing a coin flip.

Looks hard, is not. Ireland and Côte d'Ivoire use the same three colours in mirrored order — Ireland runs green to orange, Côte d'Ivoire orange to green. Norway and Iceland are the same Nordic-cross design with the red and blue swapped. Netherlands and Luxembourg differ in the shade of blue and the proportions. Australia and New Zealand both carry the Union Jack and the Southern Cross, but New Zealand shows four red stars with white borders while Australia shows six white stars, including the large Commonwealth Star beneath the Union Jack. Each of these has one rule that settles it permanently.

The odd ones out. Nepal is the only national flag that is not a rectangle — it is two stacked pennants. Switzerland and Vatican City are the only two that are officially square. These are worth knowing precisely because most quizzes cannot show them properly.

The ten-second check

  • Does it ever put Chad next to Romania? If a quiz asks you to name one of a near-identical pair in isolation, it is testing luck.
  • What aspect ratio are the flags drawn at? Most flag icon sets normalise everything to a single ratio, which quietly rewrites the two square flags and Nepal's pennant shape.
  • Does it explain the difference after a miss? “That was Côte d'Ivoire, the orange is on the hoist side” is worth more than a red cross.
  • Are the flags current? National flags do change. An undated flag set will eventually be teaching something that is no longer true.
  • Is it just an ad delivery mechanism? Flag quizzes are cheap to build, which is why so many of them are wrapped in interstitials between rounds.

One thing most flag quizzes will not tell you

Almost every flag quiz you have played showed you the wrong shape. Flag icon sets are normally distributed at a single aspect ratio — commonly 4:3 or 3:2 — because that is what makes a tidy grid. Real national flags do not share one ratio. Switzerland and Vatican City are square. Nepal is a double pennant. Several others use ratios from 1:2 to 5:8.

That is a reasonable engineering trade-off and it is worth saying out loud, because shape is part of what you are learning. If you can only recognise the Swiss flag as a wide rectangle, you have learned the icon rather than the flag.

Common questions

Which two national flags are the hardest to tell apart?

Chad and Romania. Both are vertical blue-yellow-red tricolours and the only difference is a slightly darker blue on Chad's flag, which is close to invisible at small sizes. Monaco and Indonesia are a similar case, differing mainly in proportion.

How do I tell Ireland and Côte d'Ivoire apart?

They are mirror images. Ireland runs green at the hoist to orange at the fly; Côte d'Ivoire runs orange at the hoist to green at the fly. One rule settles it permanently.

Which national flag is not rectangular?

Nepal's, which is two stacked pennants. Switzerland and Vatican City are the only two national flags that are officially square; every other national flag is a rectangle, though the ratios vary.

How do Australia's and New Zealand's flags differ?

Both show the Union Jack and the Southern Cross on blue. New Zealand's has four red stars with white borders. Australia's has six white stars, including the large Commonwealth Star below the Union Jack, and its Southern Cross includes a smaller fifth star.
