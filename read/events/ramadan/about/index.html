About Ramadan and Eid al-Fitr

Cluck follows the evening clock to learn about a month of worship and a celebration that comes after it.

How it began · Try the question

Cluck O’ClockMy friend’s family is getting a meal ready for sunset. What makes that time special?

BooklavaDuring Ramadan, sunset is when people who have been fasting can break their fast. Let’s look at what else the month means.

When is it?

Ramadan is the ninth month of the Islamic lunar calendar. It starts earlier on the Gregorian calendar each year. Communities may announce different start dates depending on how they determine the new month. Eid al-Fitr begins after Ramadan ends.

Who observes it?

Muslims around the world observe Ramadan. Many adults who are able fast from dawn to sunset. Some people are excused from fasting, and younger children are generally not expected to fast.

Why does it matter?

For Muslims, Ramadan is a holy month for worship, reflection and generosity. Many people spend extra time praying, reading the Qur’an or helping neighbours. Eid al-Fitr is a celebration at the end of the month.

How it began

Muslims remember the beginning of the Qur’an’s revelation during Ramadan. Families and communities have many ways to mark the month. The meal that breaks a day’s fast is called iftar.

Fun fact

Two different names

Iftar is the meal that breaks a day’s fast. Eid al-Fitr is the festival after the month of Ramadan.

Try it

Which word names the meal at sunset that breaks the day’s fast?

Iftar.
