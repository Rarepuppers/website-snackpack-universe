About Hanukkah

Cluck counts the nights and spots a light with a special job.

When is it?

Hanukkah begins on 25 Kislev in the Hebrew calendar and lasts eight nights. Its dates move on the Gregorian calendar, usually falling in November or December.

Who observes it?

Jewish communities around the world mark Hanukkah. Customs differ between families and places. Some gather to light the Hanukkah lamp, play dreidel or share foods cooked in oil.

Why does it matter?

Hanukkah remembers the rededication of the Temple in Jerusalem. A traditional story in the Talmud tells how oil enough for one day kept the Temple lamp burning for eight days.

How it began

The Temple rededication followed a revolt led by the Maccabees. In later generations, the account of the oil became an important way of telling the festival’s story. Today, many families add one Hanukkah light each night.

Fun fact

One light helps the others

A Hanukkah lamp is also called a hanukkiah. It has places for eight festival lights and a helper light called the shamash.
