About Lunar New Year

When is it?

Lunar New Year usually falls in January or February on the everyday Gregorian calendar. The Chinese calendar tracks both the Moon and the seasons. Check the date for the community and year you are learning about.

Who observes it?

Chinese New Year, Vietnamese Tết and Korean Seollal are examples of observances around Lunar New Year. Families in these communities and their diasporas have their own traditions.

Why does it matter?

For many families it is a time to reunite and wish each other a good year. One Chinese New Year example is a reunion dinner, where relatives may serve dishes with an extra meaning.

How it began

Families have passed down and adapted these customs over many generations. For example, some Chinese families put red paper cuttings on windows or display good wishes by the door. Traditions can look different from house to house.

Fun fact

A wish on the dinner table

Some Chinese families serve a whole fish as a wish for abundance. The words for fish and surplus sound alike in Mandarin.
